NYC Taxi Fleet Positioning

Where should a fixed fleet start a shift when missed trips and idle vehicles have different costs?

A newsvendor allocation workflow compares demand models by decision cost and distributes a constrained fleet across NYC taxi zones.

The problem

Forecast accuracy alone does not determine where vehicles should wait. Under-allocation misses trips; over-allocation leaves vehicles idle. Both must be valued under a fleet constraint.

Data & scope

The public portfolio source includes allocation code, scenario outputs and an engine audit. The separate project repository is a documentation entry point. Trip aggregates must be supplied to rerun the full pipeline.

How it works

  1. Aggregate historical trips into zone and time-window demand.
  2. Translate missed-trip and idle-vehicle costs into allocation decisions.
  3. Compare empirical, predict-then-optimize and decision-focused approaches on the same 35 scenarios.
  4. Inspect fixed-fleet and adjustable-fleet scenarios using total cost, including rental adjustments.

Key technical decision

Select models by the downstream allocation loss, not a forecast metric alone. Keep newsvendor cost separate from realized revenue and include fleet-adjustment costs in scenario comparisons.

Result & interpretation

What the evidence shows

In the committed 35-scenario bakeoff, the empirical method has the lowest mean newsvendor cost: about $369,468 per scenario. Its mean fill rate is 55.4%. These are modeled evaluation outputs, not realized savings. The dashboard shows the exact committed comparison and three stakeholder slots.

What I would improve next

The outputs are historical scenarios with modeled cost assumptions. Repositioning time, demand shifts and operational constraints require further validation. A local agent prototype exists in the source; this does not establish deployment or live dispatch use.
