Packaging Standardization — BabyShip

Seventeen strollers shipped in seventeen box sizes. Carriers bill dimensional weight, so extra empty space becomes cost, and a unique box per product is a warehouse problem.

An integer program that picks at most seven box types for 17 products, from 43 candidate boxes, while keeping the dimensional-weight penalty small.

The problem

Carriers charge dimensional weight, length × width × height ÷ 250, whenever that exceeds the product’s actual weight. A perfect box for every stroller minimizes shipping weight and maximizes the number of SKUs the warehouse has to stock, pick, and store.

Approach

  • Collected dimensions and weights for 17 strollers and 43 commercial box sizes.
  • Built a fit matrix: a stroller fits a box only if all three dimensions clear with 0.5 inch of padding.
  • Solved an integer program in Gurobi: at most 7 box types, each product assigned to exactly one feasible box, minimizing total billable weight.
  • Compared the 7-box solution with a one-box-per-product baseline.

Result

The solver selected 7 boxes. Billable weight rose from 1,613 lb to 1,718 lb, a 6.5% penalty (104.7 lb), in exchange for cutting box variety by 59%. The model has 774 binary variables and 1,394 constraints and reached a proven optimum in 0.02 seconds.

What this leaves open

The objective is billable weight, not landed dollar cost by lane or carrier contract. A next pass would price the same assignments in dollars and flag the two products that still need an oversized box.
