NCAA Selection Model

Selection committees rank teams with noisy, multi-signal résumés. The modeling question is who gets in, and then where they are seeded. Those are two different decisions.

A two-stage stacked ensemble: classify tournament selection, then predict seed for the teams the first model lets in.

The problem

A single model that tries to emit both “in or out” and a seed line mixes a classification decision with a ranking decision. Bubble teams are exactly where that mix-up is expensive.

Approach

  • Engineered 50 features from records, conference strength, NET momentum, quality wins, and strength of schedule.
  • Stacked eight tree models. A logistic regression decides selection. A ridge model predicts seed.
  • Tuned the selection cutoff on the precision-recall curve instead of using 0.5.
  • Scored the stack with 10-fold stratified cross-validation and out-of-fold predictions.

Result

Selection AUC was 0.966 at a cutoff of 0.653. The model named about 70 teams against a 68-team field. Seed predictions were fit to mean absolute error rather than to a classification score.

What this leaves open

Cross-validation here is not a pure walk-forward by season, so a later season can still influence an earlier fold. A stricter test would train only on past tournaments.
