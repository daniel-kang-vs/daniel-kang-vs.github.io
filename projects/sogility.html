Athlete Scoring — Sogility

Parents were handed a raw drill number. The old sigmoid compared every athlete on that drill with every other athlete, so a younger player was scored against older ones, and the curve flattened the players who were still developing.

A dual score for a nationwide soccer training company: a cohort sigmoid for peer standing, and a quantile benchmark for a stable performance band. Both sit on a 30–99 scale. Parent and trainer screens read the same player differently.

The problem

A score of 61 did not say whether the player was doing well, how they compared with athletes of the same age, or what to train next. Time-based drills and count-based drills also pointed in opposite directions, and the same player on the same drill jumped from attempt to attempt.

Approach

  • Kept the sigmoid. Moved its median and span from all players to the age-and-gender cohort, and blended back toward the global distribution when that cell was small.
  • Weighted the last three attempts 0.5, 0.3, and 0.2 so one noisy rep is not the score.
  • Built the performance band separately, by interpolating the 10th, 25th, 50th, 75th, and 90th percentiles, so the label stays put when the cohort changes.
  • Flipped lower-is-better drills, log-transformed skewed ones, and rescaled both outputs onto 30–99.
  • Designed a parent view around one score and five skills, and a trainer view around fast entry, comparison, and history.

Result

The number on the screen is peer standing. The band — Foundation through Elite — is the stable benchmark. The full method, the formula, and an anonymized snapshot of the session file are in the GitHub README. Player names are not published.

What this leaves open

The extract has no match result, so the scores are a measurement system, not a validated predictor of game performance. Production scoring was specified as a port into Sogility’s Elixir backend, checked against the Python reference.
