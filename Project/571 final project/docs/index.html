01 — Business Context & Stakes

Why early distress ranking matters

Lenders, insurers, and portfolio managers allocate capital against rare but costly failures. Missing a distressed firm is expensive; treating every account as high-risk is also expensive. The job is prioritization: put scarce analyst attention on the companies most likely to go bankrupt next.

Decision problem

Rank firms by bankruptcy risk so underwriting, monitoring, and workout teams can intervene earlier—tighten covenants, reduce exposure, or trigger deeper review.

Operating constraint

Only 4.3% of firms fail in a given period. Accuracy alone misleads; ranking quality (AUC) is what separates useful triage from noise.

Data reality

Ratios arrive incomplete and extreme. Up to 34% missing on key attributes. Without careful cleaning, models chase outliers instead of distress signals.

02 — Data Pipeline

Clean the ratios before you trust the score

Financial statements are messy. Extreme leverage and liquidity ratios distort tree splits; missing fields break simple averages. The pipeline treats those issues before modeling.

Step 1

Winsorization

Clip each ratio to the 0.5th–99.5th percentile so a handful of extreme values cannot dominate feature importance.

Step 2

KNN imputation

Fill gaps with k=5 nearest peers so correlated ratios stay coherent instead of collapsing to a global mean.

Step 3

Model-ready features

Only then does XGBoost see comparable, complete inputs across all 64 anonymized financial ratios.

AUC by preprocessing step

Raw data scored 0.872. Winsorization and KNN each added lift; the full pipeline reached 0.907.

End-to-end flow

03 — Methodology

XGBoost for imbalanced distress classification

The business question is ranking, so the primary metric is ROC-AUC: how well the model orders distressed firms above healthy ones. Class imbalance is treated as a design constraint, not an afterthought.

Model

Gradient-boosted trees (XGBoost) capture non-linear interactions among ratios without assuming a linear credit scorecard form.

Metric

AUC evaluates ranking quality under imbalance. A 0.90+ score means distressed firms reliably rise to the top of the risk list.

Validation

Stratified 5-fold CV keeps the bankrupt rate stable per fold, so reported performance reflects triage quality—not a lucky split.

Imbalance

With only 427 bankrupt cases vs. 9,573 survivors, preprocessing and tree-based learning matter more than oversampling theater.
