Logistics capacity allocation

Every idle cab and every missed fare is a decision.

NYC’s 13,000 yellow cabs must be pre-positioned across 263 zones before each shift. Put capacity in the wrong place and you burn money twice — empty vehicles sitting still, and riders who never get picked up.

See the $12.4M upside Explore by time slot

Business Context & Stakes

A fixed fleet. Uneven demand. Asymmetric costs.

The TLC cannot grow the medallion fleet overnight. The lever that matters is where cabs start each deployment slot. Miss a high-fare airport trip and you lose roughly $17; leave a cab idle and you waste about $12. That imbalance should pull capacity toward high-value zones — if you can see true demand.

Underage

Missed demand

Rider wants a yellow cab and none is there. Lost fare, lost service, and demand that never appears in trip logs.

Overage

Idle capacity

A cab waits where demand never arrives. Fuel, driver time, and medallion opportunity cost with no revenue.

Constraint

13,000-cab budget

Every cab assigned to Midtown is one not available in Queens. Allocation is a zero-sum logistics problem.

Asymmetric Cost per Trip

A missed Midtown fare (~$17) hurts more than an idle cab (~$12). That cost gap is why the optimizer favors airports and high-fare zones.

Data Pipeline

Yellow trips alone understate true demand

Completed yellow pickups are censored — they only count trips that found a cab. We reconstruct latent demand by combining yellow taxi records with for-hire vehicle (Uber/Lyft) activity as a proxy for unmet yellow demand, especially in the outer boroughs.

Methodology

Newsvendor optimization for capacity placement

For each deployment slot, the decision is qz: how many cabs to pre-position in zone z. The model minimizes expected business loss — idle cabs on one side, missed fares on the other — under a hard fleet budget.

01 · Sets & Variables

What We Choose

For every pickup zone \(z\), choose the number of yellow cabs to pre-position before the time slot starts.

\[ \begin{aligned} Z &= \{\text{263 TLC pickup zones}\} \\ q_z &\ge 0 \quad \text{cabs assigned to zone } z \in Z \end{aligned} \]
02 · Demand & Costs

Business Inputs

Demand is random and estimated from yellow taxi plus FHV proxy data. Costs make missed trips more expensive than idle cabs.

\[ \begin{aligned} D_z &= \text{latent demand in zone } z \\ C^u_z &= \text{underage cost (lost fare)} \\ C^o_z &= \text{overage cost (idle cab)} \end{aligned} \]
03 · Objective Function

Minimize Expected Newsvendor Cost

The first term penalizes too many cabs in a zone. The second term penalizes too few cabs when demand exceeds supply.

\[ \min_{\mathbf{q}} \quad \sum_{z \in Z} \left[ C^o_z \, \mathbb{E}\left(q_z - D_z\right)^+ + C^u_z \, \mathbb{E}\left(D_z - q_z\right)^+ \right] \]
04 · Constraints

Fleet Budget + Zone Bounds

The allocation cannot use more than 13,000 cabs. Zone-level floors preserve minimum service and caps avoid over-concentration.

\[ \begin{aligned} \sum_{z \in Z} q_z &\le F, \qquad F = 13{,}000 \\ \ell_z &\le q_z \le u_z \qquad \forall z \in Z \end{aligned} \]
Critical fractile intuition: without the shared fleet constraint, each zone gets the demand quantile \[ q^*_z = F_z^{-1}(\tau_z), \qquad \tau_z = \frac{C^u_z}{C^u_z + C^o_z}. \] When the cost of missing a fare rises, \(\tau_z\) increases, so the model assigns more cabs.

Business Impact

Same fleet. Smarter placement. Measurable upside.

Annualized revenue gain

~$12.4M/year

Versus proportional status-quo dispatch on peak Tuesday midday, optimized pre-positioning lifts slot revenue from ~$188K to ~$426K — about $12.4M per year when annualized across 52 Tuesdays, with no change to fleet size.
