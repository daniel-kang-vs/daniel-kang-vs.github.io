NYC Yellow Taxi
Fleet Optimization

NYC TLC has a fixed fleet of 13,000 yellow cabs. The question isn’t whether to deploy — it’s where to pre-position them before each shift, when every missed fare and every idle cab carry different costs.

The Problem

Two challenges before we can optimize

Trip data only records completed yellow pickups — not people who couldn’t find a cab. We reconstruct true demand using for-hire vehicle (Uber/Lyft) data, then solve a newsvendor allocation that penalizes missed high-fare trips more than idle cabs.

Optimization Model

A multi-zone newsvendor allocation problem

For each deployment slot, the decision is qz: how many cabs to pre-position in zone z. The objective balances two costs: underage (lost fare when demand exceeds supply) and overage (idle cab when supply exceeds demand).

01 · Sets & Variables

What We Choose

For every pickup zone \(z\), choose the number of yellow cabs to pre-position before the time slot starts.

\[ \begin{aligned} Z &= \{\text{263 TLC pickup zones}\} \\ q_z &\ge 0 \quad \text{cabs assigned to zone } z \in Z \end{aligned} \]
02 · Demand & Costs

Inputs to the Model

Demand is random and estimated from yellow taxi plus FHV proxy data. Costs make missed trips more expensive than idle cabs.

\[ \begin{aligned} D_z &= \text{latent demand in zone } z \\ C^u_z &= \text{underage cost (lost fare)} \\ C^o_z &= \text{overage cost (idle cab)} \end{aligned} \]
03 · Objective Function

Minimize Expected Newsvendor Cost

The first term penalizes too many cabs in a zone. The second term penalizes too few cabs when demand exceeds supply.

\[ \min_{\mathbf{q}} \quad \sum_{z \in Z} \left[ C^o_z \, \mathbb{E}\left(q_z - D_z\right)^+ + C^u_z \, \mathbb{E}\left(D_z - q_z\right)^+ \right] \]
04 · Constraints

Fleet Budget + Zone Bounds

The allocation cannot use more than 13,000 cabs. Zone-level floors preserve minimum service and caps avoid over-concentration.

\[ \begin{aligned} \sum_{z \in Z} q_z &\le F, \qquad F = 13{,}000 \\ \ell_z &\le q_z \le u_z \qquad \forall z \in Z \end{aligned} \]
Critical fractile intuition: without the shared fleet constraint, each zone gets the demand quantile \[ q^*_z = F_z^{-1}(\tau_z), \qquad \tau_z = \frac{C^u_z}{C^u_z + C^o_z}. \] When the cost of missing a fare rises, \(\tau_z\) increases, so the model assigns more cabs.
