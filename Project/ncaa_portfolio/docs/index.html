The Problem

Two linked tasks: selection, then seeding

Only ~26% of teams earn a tournament bid, so the selection model faces severe class imbalance. For teams that make it in, a second model predicts their seed (1–68). Getting the team count right matters as much as ranking — there are exactly 68 slots.

Tournament Selection (Training Set)

Most teams are not selected. The model must find the ~26% that make the bracket.

Selection Probability Distribution

Default 0.5 threshold picks too many teams (76). PR-curve calibration at 0.653 lands closer to 68.

Pipeline

From 15 raw columns to a stacked ensemble

Win-loss strings (e.g. “23–7”) were parsed into numeric features, then combined with NET rankings and quadrant records to build 50 engineered features. Eight base models feed a meta-learner trained with 10-fold cross-validation.

Top Features (Selection Model)

NET Rank dominates, but Quadrant 1 win% and overall win% add signal beyond the ranking alone.

Precision / Recall vs. Threshold

Raising the cutoff trades recall for precision — critical when only 68 teams can be selected.

Results

Stacked ensemble vs. single-model baseline

The final model selected 70 teams (vs. 68 actual bids) with AUC 0.966 — a 2.6-point lift over the single-model baseline. Seed predictions are spread realistically across all four regions.
